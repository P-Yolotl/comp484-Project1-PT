Why Reading Matters

Reading is important because it does much more than entertain us. Books can help us learn new things, improve our vocabulary, strengthen our imagination , and expose us to experiences that may be very different from our own. Reading also encourages us to slow down, focus, and think more deeply about ideas, characters, and the world around us.

One important way reading can affect a person is by helping them understand other people better. A study by David Kidd and Emanuele Castano found that participants who read literary fiction performed better on certain tests measuring their ability to recognize and understand other people's thoughts and emotions. This ability is sometimes called “theory of mind.” However, later attempts to reproduce this exact finding have produced mixed results, so it is better to see it as evidence of a possible benefit rather than proof that every book automatically makes someone more empathetic.

Another study followed 3,635 adults and found an association between regularly reading books and longer survival over the study period. The researchers also found that cognitive abilities appeared to help explain part of this relationship. Because this was an observational study, it cannot prove that reading itself caused people to live longer, but it shows that regular reading may be connected with meaningful long-term benefits.

You can read more about the study here.

For an individual person, reading can have an impact by introducing them to new perspectives, helping them reflect on their own experiences, and giving them a way to connect emotionally with characters and stories. A powerful book can challenge someone's beliefs, teach them about people they have never met, inspire them to learn about an issue, or simply provide comfort during a difficult time.

Featured Book

On Earth We're Briefly Gorgeous

by Ocean Vuong

A deeply personal novel written in the form of a letter from a son to his mother who cannot read The book explores themes of family, identity, memory, love, and the immigrant experience.

Bad Feminist

by Roxane Gay

A collection of essays about feminism, culture, race, gender, politics, and the challenges of living according to our own beliefs.

The Son of Man

by Jean-Baptiste Del Amo

A dark novel about a father and son traveling into an isolated wilderness, exploring family relationships, masculinity, violence, and nature.

Current Book Club Pick

Martyr!

by Kaveh Akbar

Our current selection follows Cyrus Shams, a young Iranian American poet and recovering addict who becomes obsessed with the idea of martyrdom while trying to understand his family’s past and the death of his mother. His search for meaning leads him toward art, faith, identity, and a mysterious dying artist whose story may be connected to his own.
